# How Hackathons Get Won

Research date: 2026-09-02. Sources: Parth Mittal's X article "How to find hackathon winning ideas" (2026-08-19), Devpost blog and help center, Chrome Built-in AI Challenge winner posts (2024, 2025), HackerEarth, dev.to.

## 1. Parth Mittal's eight rules

Parth Mittal has entered 35+ hackathons and won 15. Wins include Anthropic, Google DeepMind, Sarvam, ETHIndia. His rules, condensed:

1. **Do not ask AI for ideas.** Everyone gets the same answer. Use AI to test and expand an idea you already have.
2. **Collect inspiration over time.** Bookmark demos and launches on X and Reddit. Ideas collide later.
3. **The organizer's tech must be the hero.** Test: "Could I build my project without this? If the answer is yes, then you are building the wrong thing." Do not stack sponsors to hedge. "Simple is beautiful."
4. **It must be demo-able.** "Assume that you only have a minute." If the problem takes over 30 seconds to explain, drop it.
5. **Be early.** Build on a capability that just became possible.
6. **Use your domain knowledge.** Two kinds of projects win: exceptional idea, or exceptional execution backed by domain depth.
7. **Match the theme.** He rejected an "AI on-call engineer" because it was "not early anymore" and "a workflow."
8. **Do not be afraid.** His team are not game developers. They built games because models made it fast.

| Hackathon | Project | What it did |
|---|---|---|

### His losing examples

- Aaroh: agentic commerce with UCP, x402, trustless identity. "Already a mouthful."
- Medha: proactive Siri. "How do I show you something proactive, in a few seconds?"

## 2. Ten tactics for an online Devpost hackathon

1. Write the demo script before writing code. Time it to 60 seconds of story.
2. Make the sponsor API load-bearing. Say so in the submission text.
3. Pick a capability that is about to be possible. Not one that is common.
4. Read the rubric before the theme. Score against each criterion by name.
5. Hardcode the demo path. Mock flaky externals. Keep screenshots as backup.
6. Open the video with the pitch. No logo animation.
7. Use a screencast with narration. Not a marketing video.
8. Do not stack sponsors to hedge.
9. Spend the first 30 percent of the video on the problem. Not the stack.
10. Upload early. Reserve two to three hours before the deadline for recording and upload.

## 3. Why strong projects lose

- Scope too large for the window.
- No time saved for the video. Pitch improvised.
- Video leads with "how" instead of "what" and "why."
- Pitch ignores the named criteria.
- Sponsor tech is an add-on.
- The idea cannot be shown in seconds (proactive, ambient, cumulative).
- One-line pitch is too long.
- Dead links, rough UI, no polish.
- One person tries to cover every discipline. Result feels unfinished.

## 5. Video guidance

- Length: under 3 minutes is the rule here. Aim for 2 to 2.5 minutes.
- Structure: (1) problem in two sentences, (2) show it working, (3) impact and what comes next.
- First 10 seconds: say what it does and how it fits the hackathon. "Like a movie trailer."
- Format: screencast plus voice. Write and rehearse the script.
- Face on camera: no guidance found.
- Captions: no guidance found. Add them anyway. Judges may watch muted.
- Upload well before the deadline. Processing time varies.
